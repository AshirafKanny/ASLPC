A.  Institutional Overview
The African Sports Law and Policy Centre (ASLPC) is an independent African research, policy and knowledge institution dedicated to sports law, sports governance, sports policy and sport for development. Based in Uganda and affiliated with several international, regional and international partners and institutes, ASLPC occupies a position that no existing institution in the region currently fills: a standing, research-driven bridge between legal scholarship, public policy, sports governance and practical institutional reform.
The Centre was conceived against the backdrop of Uganda's National Sports Act, 2023, which established, for the first time, a comprehensive statutory framework for sports administration, discipline and regulation in the country. That framework created the legal architecture; it did not create the institutions, professionals and evidence base required to make it work in practice. The same gap recurs across the African continent, in governance capacity, athlete protection, sports commercialisation, integrity systems and the use of sport for development. ASLPC exists to close it.
ASLPC combines six functions that are typically dispersed across separate institutions elsewhere: a research institution, a policy think tank, a sports governance resource centre, a professional training academy, an athlete rights platform and a sport for development institution. It begins with a firm base in Uganda and East Africa and will build progressively toward a continental and international footprint, in the manner of comparable institutions such as the Sports Law and Policy Centre (Bengaluru), the Danish Institute for Sports Studies and its Play the Game initiative, and the African Union Sports Council's own research and policy work.

B.  Who We Are
The African Sports Law and Policy Centre (ASLPC) is an independent, Uganda-based African institution dedicated to research, policy development and professional practice in sports law, governance, integrity, institutional reform, professionalisation and sport for development. The Centre generates knowledge, promotes stakeholder awareness and supports evidence-based reforms aimed at strengthening Africa’s sports sector.
ASLPC is not a university department, law firm, sports federation or government agency. It maintains its own institutional identity, governance, research agenda, publications and intellectual independence. While based in Uganda, the Centre is designed to work across Africa and to respond flexibly to emerging developments and challenges within the sports industry.
The Centre seeks to build academic and professional affiliations and strategic partnerships with universities, research institutions, professional bodies, sports organisations, policymakers, regulators and international organisations at local, regional, continental and global levels. Through these relationships, ASLPC will promote collaborative research, knowledge exchange, professional capacity building, policy dialogue and innovation while preserving its independence and positioning itself as a credible African hub for sports law, policy, governance, integrity and sport for development.
C.  Why the Centre Matters
Sport in Africa has outgrown the informal and purely recreational frame in which it was long understood. It is now a field of law, a regulatory and governance concern, a commercial industry, a vehicle for social and economic development, an instrument of public policy and, increasingly, a site of diplomacy and continental cooperation. Africa's sports industry has been valued at roughly US$12–15 billion and is projected to grow at close to 8 percent annually, yet it contributes only a fraction of the share of GDP recorded in more mature sporting economies, held back in significant part by governance weaknesses, thin regulatory capacity and limited investment-ready institutions.
Uganda's National Sports Act, 2023 makes the timing of ASLPC's establishment deliberate rather than opportunistic. The legal framework now exists; the credible, independent, evidence-generating institution needed to make it work in practice does not. No institution currently combines rigorous legal research, policy translation, governance support, professional training and sport-for-development practice under one roof, and specifically oriented to African and Ugandan realities.
The Gap ASLPC Addresses
•	Universities teach sports law as a subject but rarely operate as standing research-to-policy institutions.
•	Law firms handle sports disputes and transactions but do not generate public research or convene the sector.
•	Federations and government agencies regulate sport but are rarely positioned to produce independent, evidence-based analysis of their own governance.
•	Conventional think tanks address public policy broadly but seldom develop the sport-specific expertise that regulatory reform, athlete protection and sports commercialisation now require.
ASLPC is designed to sit deliberately in the space between these institutions, connecting academic research with practical policy, legal practice, sports administration and community development in a single, coordinated institution.
D.  Vision
A legally sound, well-governed, inclusive, commercially sustainable and socially transformative African sports sector, where law, policy and evidence not improvisation shape how sport is governed, protected, developed and harnessed for sustainable development.
E.  Mission
ASLPC advances its vision through rigorous research, evidence based policy development, legal and institutional reform, professional capacity building, stakeholder engagement and sport-for-development initiatives. The Centre works with governments, sports federations, athletes, academia, the private sector, civil society and development partners to generate and translate knowledge into practical reforms that strengthen the governance, integrity, inclusiveness, professionalism and sustainable development of Africa’s sports sector.
F.  Institutional Purpose
ASLPC exists to generate African sports law and sports policy knowledge grounded in Ugandan and continental realities, and to convert that knowledge into usable outcomes: legislative and regulatory improvement, stronger sports governance, better-protected athletes, more investable sports businesses, more capable sports professionals, and measurable social outcomes from sport for development practice. Its purpose is not to produce research for its own sake, but to be the institution that makes Africa's evolving sports-law frameworks including Uganda's National Sports Act, 2023  work in practice.
G.  Core Values
1. Integrity & Accountability. We uphold honesty, ethical conduct, transparency and accountability in our research, advice, partnerships and institutional operations. Our work is rigorous, defensible and open to scrutiny.

2. Independence & African Ownership. We protect our intellectual, editorial and institutional independence while advancing African expertise, leadership and ownership of sports law and policy knowledge. Our work remains free from undue influence by funders, governments, federations or other interests.

3. Excellence & Evidence. We pursue the highest standards of research, professional practice and knowledge production, grounding our analysis and recommendations in evidence, law, data and comparative experience while embracing innovation and new approaches.

4. Inclusion, Equality & Human Dignity. We promote equality, diversity, inclusion and respect for human rights, ensuring that athletes, women, youth, persons with disabilities and marginalised communities are recognised, heard and able to benefit from sport and sports-sector development.

5. Impact & Transformation. We are committed to translating knowledge into practical institutional, professional and social change. We measure our success not only by research and activities, but by their contribution to better governance, stronger institutions, improved sporting systems and meaningful community outcomes.
H.  Strategic Objectives
ASLPC's mission translates into five strategic objectives, each anchored in a specific institutional function.
•	Advance Sports Law, Research and Policy Development. To generate high-quality, publishable and policy-relevant research on sports law, regulation and policy, and provide evidence based technical support to governments, regulators and sports organisations in the development, review and reform of sports laws and policies. 
•	Strengthen Sports Governance, Integrity and Athlete Protection. To promote good governance, institutional accountability, ethical conduct, integrity, safeguarding and athlete rights across the sports sector, including athlete welfare, fair contracting, image rights and complementary support to existing anti-doping and anti-corruption frameworks. 
•	Build Capacity and Professionalise Africa’s Sports Sector. To strengthen the legal, managerial and professional capacity of sports stakeholders through structured education, training and knowledge development, while supporting the professionalisation and commercialisation of sport, including intellectual property, broadcasting rights and other emerging areas of the sports business ecosystem. 
•	Leverage Sport for Inclusive Social and Economic Development. To advance the use of sport as a vehicle for social inclusion, youth empowerment, community development and economic transformation, aligned with Uganda’s national development priorities and broader African development frameworks. 
•	Foster Strategic Partnerships, Dialogue and African Sports Knowledge. To establish platforms and strategic partnerships that connect governments, regulators, federations, athletes, lawyers, academia, business, civil society and international institutions, while developing and disseminating African sports-law and policy knowledge through research repositories, publications, policy dialogues and collaborative initiatives.

J.  Research and Policy Agenda
Research is one of ASLPC's principal functions and the evidentiary foundation for everything else the Centre does. The Centre's research agenda is organised around pillars that are simultaneously relevant to Africa, responsive to Uganda's sports environment, internationally competitive, capable of generating academic publications, capable of informing public policy, attractive to development partners and funders, and capable of producing practical solutions for sports organisations.
Research Pillars and Representative Questions
•	Sports Governance and Regulatory Design
How should Uganda and comparable African jurisdictions structure the relationship between government regulators, federations and independent dispute-resolution bodies under new sports legislation?
•	Athlete Rights, Integrity and Safeguarding 
What legal and institutional protections do African athletes have against match-fixing, exploitation and abuse, and how do these compare with international standards?
•	Sports Commercialisation and Intellectual Property 
How adequately do Ugandan and African law protect image rights, sponsorship arrangements and broadcasting rights in a rapidly commercialising sports economy?
•	Sports Dispute Resolution and Arbitration.
What access do African athletes and federations have to efficient, affordable and impartial sports arbitration, and what role could a regional mechanism play?
•	Sports Taxation and Financial Regulation 
How does the current tax and financial-regulatory treatment of athletes, clubs and federations in Uganda compare with regional practice, and what reforms would support sector growth?
•	Sports Technology and Digital Transformation 
What legal and regulatory issues arise from the growth of esports, sports data and digital broadcasting in African markets?
•	Gender, Disability and Inclusion in Sport 
What legal and policy barriers limit women's, girls' and persons-with-disabilities' full participation in African sport, and how are comparable jurisdictions addressing them?
•	Sport for Development and Social Impact 
What evidence exists that sport-for-development programming in Uganda and the region produces measurable social outcomes, and how can that evidence base be strengthened?
This agenda is reviewed and prioritised annually, coordinated across all four divisions through the standing African Sports Law Research Program  (see Section K), and calibrated each year to the issues of greatest relevance to Uganda and East Africa while contributing to a wider, continental body of African sports-law scholarship.
K.  Programme and Thematic Divisions
ASLPC is organised around four complementary technical divisions, each deliberately scoped to avoid functional overlap with the others. Cross-cutting themes   athlete rights, integrity and match-fixing, and intellectual property   are anchored in a lead division but drawn on by the others rather than duplicated across all four.
Division A: Sports Law Research and Legal Development
The Centre's principal knowledge-generation and scholarly research arm, producing rigorous, publishable and policy-relevant legal research on Uganda's and Africa's sport sector, spanning comparative sports law, commercial and broadcasting rights, sporting intellectual property, athlete rights and match-fixing, arbitration, anti-doping regulation, and sports taxation and digital sport.
Division B: Sports Policy and Governance
Translates the Centre's research into practical governance and policy solutions, supporting the implementation of the National Sports Act, 2023 and comparable regional frameworks through legislative analysis and drafting support, governance audits and standards, institutional-design advisory work, and integrity and safeguarding policy support delivered alongside not in place of mandated anti-doping and law-enforcement bodies.
Division C: Sport for Development and Social Impact
Promotes and evaluates the use of sport as a tool for youth development, education, employment, gender equality, social inclusion, peace building, disability inclusion and health, in line with the Sustainable Development Goals and African Union development priorities, including a Uganda Sport-for-Development policy and impact framework adapting established international indicator methodologies to a Ugandan context.
Division D: Education, Training and Professional Development
Branded publicly as the ASLPC Academy, this division delivers practical professional education in sports law, governance, integrity, safeguarding and sport-for-development practice complementing, rather than duplicating, the coaching and technical training already run by national federations through short courses, executive workshops, and a phased pathway toward accredited programs delivered with university partners.
The Centre's structure follows simple three-tier logic: a Board of Directors provides strategic oversight and safeguards independence; an Executive Director leads implementation; the four divisions carry out the Centre's technical work; and three shared functions   Finance and Administration, Partnerships and Resource Mobilisation, and Communications and Knowledge Management  service all four divisions rather than being duplicated within them. An Advisory Council or Fellows Network of respected academics, judges, lawyers, sports administrators, athletes, policymakers and international experts complements this structure in a purely advisory capacity.
L.  Flagship Programs and Initiatives
Beyond its four divisions, ASLPC operates a small number of standing, cross-divisional programs that function as durable institutional platforms rather than one-off events, complemented by an indicative annual calendar of flagship convenings.
Standing Platforms
•	African Sports Law Research Program. The Centre's standing annual research agenda, coordinated across all four divisions.
•	African Sports Governance Observatory. A standing monitoring platform, built on Division B's governance standard, tracking governance, regulation, accountability and institutional performance across African sport over time (in the tradition of comparable tools such as the Sports Governance Observer developed internationally by Play the Game and KU Leuven).
•	Sports Law Clinic. a supervised platform for legal education and selected public-interest sports-law assistance, feeding Division D's research-training-practice pipeline.
•	Athlete Rights and Safeguarding Program. A standing research, awareness and capacity-building function on athlete protection, drawing jointly on Divisions A and B.
•	African Sports Law Database. A digital repository of African sports legislation, policy instruments, regulations, cases and governance materials, maintained by Division A and used across the Centre.
•	ASLPC Academy. The Centre's standing professional-education platform, through which all training programming is delivered and progressively accredited.
Indicative Annual Calendar

M.  Sport for Development and Social Impact
ASLPC treats sport for development as an evidence discipline, not only an advocacy cause. Division C develops a Uganda Sport-for-Development policy and impact framework, adapting established international indicator methodologies in the tradition of UNESCO's Kazan Action Plan follow-up work and the African Union Sports Council's continental pillars to a Ugandan indicator set capable of tracking real outcomes in youth development, education, employment, gender equality, social inclusion, peace building, disability inclusion and health.
The division's work is delivered through cross-sectoral pilot partnerships combining sport with education, health, gender and youth programming; rights, safeguarding and inclusion support for participants in sport-for-development programming; and a Sport for Development Community Program delivered with local and international partners, with impact measured and published rather than merely asserted.
N.  Training and Capacity Development
The ASLPC Academy (Division D) is the Centre's professional-education platform, delivering short courses, seminars and executive workshops in sports law, governance, athlete rights, contracts, intellectual property, integrity and dispute resolution to federation officials, club administrators, coaches, athletes, lawyers, government officials and students.
Training content is deliberately linked to the Centre's own research through a shared curriculum and quality-assurance framework, and the Academy pursues a phased accreditation and certification strategy, moving from non-credit professional courses toward accredited programs delivered through university partnership. A pipeline links research fellows and student researchers into the Academy's teaching faculty, and the Academy benchmarks itself against comparable continental training institutions such as the CAF Football Management Executive Program.
O.  Publications and Knowledge Dissemination
Knowledge dissemination is central to ASLPC's public value. The Centre's principal publication vehicles include peer-reviewed journal articles and books; regular policy briefs on live legislative and regulatory questions; an annual African Sports Law and Policy Report documenting major developments, challenges and reform priorities across the continent; a State of Sports Governance report from Division B; and the African Sports Law Database, a maintained digital repository of Ugandan and comparative African sports-law materials, legislation, regulations, cases and governance instruments, communicated and managed by the Centre's Communications and Knowledge Management function.
P.  Policy Engagement and Advocacy
ASLPC engages policy processes as an independent, evidence-based contributor rather than as an advocacy organisation for any single interest. Division B provides policy and legislative analysis, and drafting or review support, to regulators and legislatures; conducts governance audits and maintains a published governance standard and self-assessment toolkit for federations and clubs; supports institutional design, including the functional separation of regulatory administration from independent dispute resolution; and convenes a standing multi-stakeholder advisory forum with defined athlete representation.
This engagement is deliberately structured to preserve the Centre's independence: professional and program partnerships with federations, government agencies and development partners may co-design or co-fund specific work without acquiring editorial control over ASLPC's broader findings or public positions (see Section Q).
Q.  Partnerships and Institutional Collaboration
ASLPC's credibility depends on convening a genuinely broad coalition rather than becoming the instrument of any single actor. The Centre builds structured, differentiated relationships across the following categories:
•	Government. Ministry of Education and Sports, Ministry of Gender, Labour and Social Development, National Council of Sports, and comparable regulators elsewhere in East Africa and Africa.
•	Sports bodies. National and international federations, the Uganda Olympic Committee, the National Anti-Doping Organisation, and the African Union Sports Council.
•	Legal and dispute-resolution institutions. The Judiciary, the Uganda Law Society, the Uganda Law Reform Commission, and the Court of Arbitration for Sport.
•	Universities and research institutions. universities, regional law faculties and international sports-law research networks.
•	Development and international partners. Bodies such as the Commonwealth Secretariat, UNESCO, relevant UN agencies, and comparable institutions internationally, such as the Sports Law and Policy Centre, Bengaluru.
•	Private sector and media. Law firms and professional-services firms, corporate sponsors and investors, technology companies, and media and broadcasting organisations.
•	Civil society and communities. Athletes and athlete associations, youth and community organisations, and civil-society sports bodies.
The value proposition for each category is deliberate: government and regulators gain independent, evidence-based analysis they cannot easily produce internally; federations gain governance tools and integrity support; universities gain a research and training partner without absorbing operational risk; development partners gain a credible, Africa-rooted delivery institution; and the private sector gains reliable commercial and intellectual-property research supporting sports investment. This structured, differentiated approach to partnership is itself modelled on institutions such as Play the Game/the Danish Institute for Sports Studies, which pairs government-backed hosting with a wide, self-governing international network of academic and civil-society partners.

S.  Geographical Scope
ASLPC begins with a firm base in Uganda and East Africa, where its founding research, partnerships and programming are concentrated, before progressively developing an African and international footprint through research networks, comparative publications, continental convenings and selective regional partnerships. This staged geography is deliberate: it allows the Centre to build institutional credibility and a track record of impact at home before expanding its ambitions, rather than spreading a founding institution thinly across the continent from the outset.
T.  Institutional Growth Strategy
Phase I : Establishment and Foundation
Legal establishment and governance structures; core staffing; agreement and setting of the first research agenda; a website and digital identity; initial partnerships with Uganda Pentecostal University's Faculty of Law and government; a public launch; and the first sensitisation and research programs.
Phase II : Expansion and Institutional Consolidation
Launch of the ASLPC Academy's professional training; delivery of major research projects and the first annual conference; establishment of the African Sports Governance Observatory; launch of the Athlete Rights and Safeguarding Program and the first Sport for Development pilot partnerships; and expanded partnerships across East Africa.
Phase III : Continental and International Expansion
Development of African research networks and continental programming; completion and public launch of the African Sports Law Database; new international partnerships; accredited professional programs where institutionally appropriate; regional program hubs where justified by demand; and publication of major African sports-law indices and reports.
Financial Sustainability
ASLPC pursues a diversified sustainability model from the outset, distinguishing public-interest activities that typically require grant funding from income-generating activities that contribute directly to institutional sustainability.
•	Public-interest sources: research grants from universities and international funders; international development and technical-assistance grants; government-supported programs linked to the National Sports Act, 2023; and foundation funding for governance, integrity and sport-for-development work.
•	Income-generating sources: consultancy and advisory services; professional training and short courses through the ASLPC Academy; conferences, symposia and event sponsorship; membership or subscription programs, including federation subscriptions to the governance-support service; commissioned research fees; publication and knowledge products; corporate sponsorship; and, over time, an endowment or long-term funding mechanism.
No single donor, sponsor or government relationship is permitted to constitute more than a defined share of the Centre's income a discipline consistent with how comparable independent institutions, such as the Danish Institute for Sports Studies, combine a core public grant with diversified project and partnership funding while preserving editorial independence.
Monitoring, Evaluation and Impact
ASLPC tracks its performance at three distinct levels outputs, outcomes and long-term impact rather than measuring activity alone: outputs such as publications, training delivered and knowledge resources published; outcomes such as policies influenced, governance audits completed, athletes and communities reached, and partnerships and grants secured; and long-term impact such as demonstrable improvement in federation governance standards, sustained policy uptake evidenced by citation in legislative processes, measurable social outcomes from sport-for-development pilots, and recognition as a credible reference institution by African and international peers.
U.  Institutional Proposition
No existing institution in Uganda or the wider East African region combines all of the functions ASLPC proposes. Universities teach and occasionally research sports law but do not operate as standing policy institutions. Law firms transact and litigate but do not publish independent research or convene the sector. Federations and government agencies regulate but are rarely positioned to critique their own governance. Conventional think tanks address public policy broadly but lack sport-specific expertise.
ASLPC's distinctive advantage is that it integrates these functions in a single institution, allowing it to generate African sports-law knowledge grounded in Ugandan and regional realities; convert research directly into policy and legislative recommendations; support institutional and governance reform with practical tools, not only critique; build professional capacity through training linked to its own research; connect law with sports business and commercialisation; protect athlete interests across legal, governance and safeguarding functions; promote sport for development with measurable, evidence-based methods; convene diverse stakeholders around a shared, credible evidence base; and build African expertise and leadership in a field still dominated by external models.
The African Sports Law and Policy Centre is intended to become more than a conventional sports-law research organisation. It is designed to emerge as an African platform for generating knowledge, shaping policy, strengthening sports institutions, protecting athletes, developing professionals, supporting sustainable sports businesses and demonstrating the developmental power of sport a credible, scalable, financially sustainable and distinctly African institution, drawing selectively on international best practice while remaining firmly rooted in African and Ugandan realities. This profile is presented as the foundation for ASLPC's website, institutional brochure, concept notes, partnership and funding proposals, research proposals, conference materials and stakeholder presentations, and is offered for consideration by founding partners, universities, government institutions, sports organisations, donors, development partners, corporate sponsors, and legal and professional institutions.
